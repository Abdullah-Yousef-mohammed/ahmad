First market

A shared wallet beside the records Malaysia already has.

The Malaysian Qualifications Register lists accredited programmes, and universities such as UTAR and UTM already run their own certificate checks. EduChain Verify does not replace MQA or those records.

What is already there

MQA explains that it does not itself verify every person’s qualification. Some universities issue blockchain certificates or QR checks on their own portals. A graduate can still end up with a different link for every campus.

What this adds

Participating institutions, graduates, and employers use one reusable wallet. A graduate shares a selected credential. An employer sees the issuer, the status, and the proof. Verified skills can be matched to a job description only with the learner’s permission, and the result stays explainable.

1

Blockchain

Store proofs, identifiers, and status events. Keep the personal file in protected storage.

2

Smart contracts

Apply the institution’s issuance and revocation rules. The university still decides who graduates.

3

Digital identity

Authenticate students, staff, and employers, and stop an ordinary account from issuing awards.

4

Artificial intelligence

Help read older documents and compare verified skills with a role. People can override a bad suggestion.

5

Digital ownership

The graduate controls what is shared. The awarding institution remains the authority for the academic claim.

A pilot of 6 to 12 months would start with a small group of institutions and employers, agree consent and issuer checks first, and compare verification time with the current manual process. English and Bahasa Malaysia interfaces are part of the launch plan. A ledger proves a record was not changed after it was written. It does not, by itself, prove the original award was true.
